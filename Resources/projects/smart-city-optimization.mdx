## Overview

A graph-based routing and connectivity system applied to Egyptian city infrastructure, modeled as weighted graphs of districts and connection points.

## Approach

- Modeled the city as a graph where districts are nodes and candidate connections are weighted edges.
- **Dijkstra's algorithm** for shortest-path routing between any two districts, handling realistic traffic-style weights.
- **Minimum Spanning Tree (MST)** to find the cheapest way to connect all districts with minimal total infrastructure cost.

## Implementation

- Pure Python implementation of Dijkstra and Kruskal/Prim-style MST from scratch.
- Command-line interface to load city data, query routes, and visualize connectivity decisions.

## Key Takeaways

- Choosing the right graph algorithm depends on the objective: cheapest full coverage (MST) vs. fastest single route (Dijkstra).
- Graph modeling skills translate directly to network design, logistics, and infrastructure planning problems.
